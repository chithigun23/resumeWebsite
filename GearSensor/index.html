Gear position system

An Arduino UNO R4 WiFi reads the shift actuator sensor and the neutral switch, works out which gear the car is in, and sends that gear to the MoTeC M1 GPRP as an analog voltage. This page covers the physical layout, the firmware structure and a draft FMEA.

  • Firmware v2 compiles for the UNO R4 WiFi, with and without CAN, WiFi and Bluetooth
  • 29 desktop test groups pass (state machine, fault reasons, CAN decode, estimate, LED display, phone data, the real threshold margins from the hand-shift log, and the 300 ms shift lockout)
  • 300 ms shift lockout (2026-11-11): the Arduino counted two shifts very close together once, so no new shift event can start within 300 ms of the last, and the next needs a fresh crossing. Running on the bench board
  • Android app built and run on a phone, with demo data
  • Gear model fitted to the car (2026-11-11): a full 1-2 or 2-1 stroke skips neutral, the neutral switch must stay closed 300 ms to count
  • Serial at 921600 baud: the status line costs 1.8 ms instead of 175 ms. Phone data every 50 ms with the LPS range
  • Loop timing measured on the bench board with WiFi, Bluetooth and the fast log on: worst gap 15 ms idle, 36 to 47 ms with a Bluetooth subscriber
  • Thresholds set from the third car log (2026-11-11, 60 s at 921600 baud, no logging stalls, worst gap per second 15 to 17 ms, fastlog_aborted 0): upshift 3.10 V (was 3.0), downshift 1.775 V (was 1.8), and a downshift must hold 30 ms (an upshift still 15 ms). All 5 upshifts (N to 6th) and the 6-5, 5-4 and 4-3 downshifts were counted

  • Radio work still takes up to about 45 ms in the loop. Drive with the radios off until sampling moves to a timer interrupt

Physical architecture

The M1 drives the actuator and decides when a shift ends. The Arduino sits beside it as the gear position sensor: it watches the actuator move and reports the new gear, which closes the loop.

One shift, step by step

  1. The driver pulls a paddle. The M1 energises the up or down solenoid. The manual suggests a shift time near 150 ms.
  2. The actuator moves the selector and the LPS leaves its 1.775 to 3.10 V rest band.
  3. After the LPS has stayed past a threshold for 15 ms (30 ms for a downshift), the Arduino counts one shift and moves one gear. 1 and 2 swap places and neutral is skipped, because a full stroke passes neutral without stopping there. From N an upshift lands in 2 and a downshift in 1. The output changes to the new gear voltage. The neutral switch flashes closed during a 1-2 or 2-1 stroke (75 ms measured) and that is ignored, since it must stay closed 300 ms to count. A shift takes about 300 ms, so no other shift can be counted for 300 ms after this one.
  4. The M1 sees the gear change and ends the shift.
  5. The Arduino waits for the LPS to return to rest and checks the neutral switch from 100 ms after the shift. A shift never lands in N, so a neutral switch that is still closed 800 ms after the shift means the shift did not land, and the gear is set to N. Then it accepts the next shift, but only once 300 ms have passed since the last one (SHIFT_LOCKOUT_MS) and the LPS has been seen on the rest side of the threshold after that. A bounce back over a threshold during the shift, or one still held past 300 ms, is the same shift and is not counted again. The output keeps the new gear voltage while it waits.

From the LPS crossing a threshold to the output changing takes about 15 to 20 ms. That has to stay well inside the M1 shift time.

What the M1 expects

  • The M1 reads the gear position sensor at high speed and ends a shift as soon as the gear changes.
  • Each gear has a calibrated position and tolerance in the M1. The Arduino voltage table has to match what the M1 learned.
  • A voltage outside the M1 diagnostic limits for longer than its diagnostic delay counts as a sensor fault, and the M1 falls back to its own gear estimate from wheel and engine speed.
  • The M1 has no support for half shifts, such as finding neutral on a motorbike gearbox. The driver handles N to 1 with the clutch. Neutral is only reached by a half shift, usually by hand, so the paddles move 1 to 2 and 2 to 1 directly.

Code architecture

The Arduino sketch only reads pins, writes the DAC, draws the LED matrix and serves the phone link. All decisions and data formats live in plain C++ headers with no Arduino dependency (state machine, CAN decode, LED display, phone data), so a PC can test them.

Who is trusted for what

Neutral switchAccurate for whether the car is in neutral, once it has stayed closed for 300 ms. It flashes closed on every 1-2 and 2-1 shift (75 ms measured) without the box staying in neutral, so shorter closures are ignored. It sets the gear to N and overrides the tracked gear in every running mode. A shift that ends with it still closed 800 ms later did not land, and the gear becomes N.
LPSReliable for when a shift happened. In the third car log (hand shifts only) downshift dips reached 1.72 to 1.745 V and stayed below the threshold for 90 to 565 ms, against a rest of 1.81 to 1.88 V, so 1.775 V has only about 30 to 35 mV each side and a downshift must hold 30 ms. Upshift peaks reached 3.29 to 3.35 V against a rest of 2.90 to 2.92 V, so 3.10 V is about 0.18 V from each. Both thresholds are provisional until a log with the actuator fitted. It drives every shift event, and a shift always moves one gear and never lands in N. The furthest voltage of each shift is logged.
Gear estimateFrom CAN engine speed, wheel speed and clutch. A cross-check only, off until calibrated. A sustained estimate that disagrees replaces the tracked gear.

Builds

  • Default build, with WiFi, Bluetooth and the fast log: 114384 bytes flash (43%), 14000 bytes RAM (42%).
  • For the car, without the radios and the fast log, with -DGPS_WIFI_ENABLED=0 -DGPS_BLE_ENABLED=0 -DGPS_FASTLOG=0: 58256 bytes flash (22%), 7056 bytes RAM (21%).
  • GPS_FASTLOG is on by default and adds about 3 KB flash and 2.5 KB RAM. It keeps the last 256 LPS samples (about 1.3 s at 5 ms) and prints them as CSV lines F,ms,lps_mV,neutral,mode,gear after a gear, mode or neutral change or an LPS swing of 0.35 V or more. Serial.write blocks and Serial.availableForWrite() returns 0 on this core, so the dump works from a snapshot, writes two lines per loop, and abandons a dump if one write takes over 3 ms (counter fastlog_aborted in the status line). Use a capture tool that reads in bulk. Build with -DGPS_FASTLOG=0 once the thresholds are set.
  • CAN, with GPS_CAN_ENABLED=1, adds about 4 KB. Uses the Arduino_CAN library that ships with the R4 core.
  • Bluetooth uses the ArduinoBLE library (2.1.0), which pulled in Arduino_SpiNINA as a dependency.
  • DEBUG is on. It prints at 921600 baud on state changes and once a second, including the furthest LPS voltage of the last shift, max_gap_ms, gap_since_last_line_ms (worst gap between samples since the previous line) and fastlog_aborted. Open the serial monitor at 921600. Set DEBUG to false for the car.

Phone display

A diagnostics view for the bench and the car. The board makes its own WiFi network and also sends the same data over Bluetooth LE. It shows what the code thinks and why, so a fault can be read without a laptop. It does not change what the board sends to the M1.

What it shows

  • The gear in a big ring (X when not known, the same symbol as the LED matrix), and the state: UNKNOWN, ARMED, SHIFTING or FAULT.
  • Gear by LPS count beside gear by CAN estimate, and whether they agree. The estimate shows even while the logic does not use it.
  • CAN status: not built in, started, frames seen but signals stale, or signals fresh.
  • A plain-language reason whenever the gear is not known or in FAULT.
  • LPS voltage on a bar with the 1.78 V down and 3.1 V up thresholds (1.775 V in the firmware) and the 1.78 to 3.1 V rest band, neutral switch, output to the M1, last shift peak, a log of changes, and the longest gap between samples.
  • An amber range box on the LPS bar, and "(range x to y)" next to the voltage: the lowest and highest LPS since the previous update. The board samples every 5 ms but the phone updates every 50 to 120 ms, so without this a fast stroke could fall between two updates. The box shows when the range is 40 mV or more.

Ways to connect

  • WiFi page. Join GearSensor and open http://192.168.4.1. Works on any phone.
  • Android app. Built and installed from the android folder. It scans for the board and reconnects by itself.
  • Hosted web app. chithigunatilake.com/GearSensor/live/, unlisted. Web Bluetooth needs HTTPS, so the board cannot serve this one itself.
  • Demo. The Demo button plays start-up, shifts up to 6th, an estimate disagreement on 5th, shifts back down with 2 to 1 skipping neutral, a hand shift into neutral, and the two faults (reasons 4 and 5). It no longer plays the retired reason 2.
  • Fast LPS log. Separate from the phone page. The board prints short LPS bursts on the serial port around each change, to capture exact curves for setting thresholds.

Bluetooth data

Service 7a1c0001-5e6b-4b0a-9a53-67656172536e, characteristic 7a1c0002-..., read and notify, 20 bytes, little endian. Bytes 16 and 17 used to hold the CAN frame count. The WiFi JSON has the range as lo and hi in millivolts and still has the frame count as cf.

What it costs

  • Every radio call is a round trip to the radio module, and it shares the loop with the 5 ms sampling. Requests are limited to one per 50 ms (was 100) and a slow client is dropped after 40 ms. Radios are serviced only when no shift is in progress.
  • Measured on the bench board (worst gap between samples in each second, gap_since_last_line_ms): idle 175 ms before the baud fix and 15 ms after. With a Bluetooth subscriber receiving 20 notifications a second, 176 to 222 ms before and 36 to 47 ms after. The longest single radio step was about 42 ms.
  • The page shows the longest gap between samples. For the car build with -DGPS_WIFI_ENABLED=0 -DGPS_BLE_ENABLED=0 -DGPS_FASTLOG=0. A proposed fix, not done: move the sampling, state machine and DAC output into a timer interrupt so radio work cannot delay them.
  • WiFi and Bluetooth start-up adds a few seconds at boot, while the gear is not known anyway.
  • Neither link can send anything to the board. The page is read only.

Gaps found while writing this

  • A neutral switch stuck closed is not caught. The code trusts the switch, so a valid estimate in gear cannot overrule it.
  • Version 1 read the DAC output back on A1. Version 2 dropped that, so a wrong output voltage is not detected.
  • A floating or unpowered LPS reads mid-range and passes the range check. It only shows up as a 2 second release timeout, which the phone display now explains.
  • The M1 diagnostic limits and per-gear positions have to be set from the Arduino output before any of this is meaningful.

Open items

  • CAN wiring. The transceiver is wired with TX on D10, which is the R4 CAN TX pin. D3 has no CAN function, so the receive line has to be on D13, the only header pin with CAN RX. If D3 is the transceiver standby or enable pin, the sketch does not drive it yet.
  • CAN setup. Still needs the bus bit rate and the frame layout for engine speed, wheel speed and clutch position. CAN stays off until then, because a node at the wrong bit rate can disturb the M1 bus.
  • Estimate calibration. Measure RATIO_SCALE, wheel speed units, clutch position scale and minimum speeds from a logged run, then enable it.
  • LPS. The bench reading near 1.0 V is the unpowered sensor with the car off. Confirm supply and output range from the Haltech spec sheet and confirm the mount.
  • Thresholds are provisional. 3.10 V up, 1.775 V down and a 30 ms downshift filter are centred in the gaps measured in the third car log. Every shift in all three car logs was a hand shift, because the paddle shift actuator is not fitted, and the LPS stays where the hand leaves it. Run the fast LPS log (on in the default build) at 921600 baud with a bulk-reading capture over many shifts with the actuator fitted, then set the thresholds again from the traces and build with -DGPS_FASTLOG=0.
  • Downshift margin. Rest after a downshift was 1.81 to 1.88 V and downshift dips reached 1.72 to 1.745 V (held below the threshold for 90 to 565 ms), so 1.775 V has only about 30 to 35 mV each side. Rest after an upshift was 2.90 to 2.92 V and upshift peaks 3.29 to 3.35 V, so 3.10 V has about 0.18 V each side. The first log never detected a downshift at 1.5 V. With the actuator fitted the LPS will likely rest nearer the centre. Check that a rest after a downshift never reads as a second downshift.
  • Third car log, unexplained 3rd gear stretch. After the 4-3 downshift the board stayed in 3rd from about 42 s to 48 s and the LPS did not go below 2.0 V until a hand shift into neutral. The user has not yet said what was done there, so it is unexplained.
  • Neutral switch chatter on entry. In the third log the switch closed for 24 ms, opened for 80 ms, then closed, and N showed 360 ms after it finally closed. Check that behaviour again on hand shifts into neutral.
  • 1-2 shift not yet seen with the new code. No 1-2 shift happened in the third log, so the neutral flash handling is still only tested on the PC.
  • Neutral switch flash. The switch closes briefly on every 1-2 and 2-1 shift (one complete flash measured 75 ms), which is what made all 8 first-log upshifts from 1st look like a shift into neutral. It must now stay closed 300 ms (NEUTRAL_ACCEPT_MS). Measure the flash length over many shifts to confirm the margin. A hand shift into neutral now shows N about 0.3 s late. Check that is acceptable.
  • Timer interrupt sampling. Radio work still takes up to about 45 ms in the loop. Moving the sampling, state machine and DAC output into a timer interrupt would stop it delaying them. Proposed, not done. Until then drive with the radios off.
  • WiFi and Bluetooth. The 2026-11-11 rate tests ran with both built in and a Bluetooth subscriber receiving 20 notifications a second, but the client used is not recorded here. The Android app has run only with demo data in earlier checks, so its Bluetooth link, long sessions and automatic reconnect are untested against the board.
  • M1 setup. Set the gear position diagnostic limits and delay so 0.0 V is a fault, and learn each gear voltage from the Arduino output.
  • Timing. Measure the delay from the LPS crossing to the M1 seeing the new gear, against the M1 shift time.
  • Firmware. The default build has WiFi, Bluetooth, the fast LPS log and DEBUG on, with CAN off. The 2026-11-11 rate tests ran with those on at 921600 baud. For the car set DEBUG to false and build with -DGPS_WIFI_ENABLED=0 -DGPS_BLE_ENABLED=0 -DGPS_FASTLOG=0. Open serial tools at 921600 baud.
